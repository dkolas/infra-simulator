# Choosing a design direction

Use this when the Project direction section in SKILL.md is still TODO, or when a new surface needs its own treatment.

## Process

1. **Ground in the subject.** What is this product for, who uses it, and in what setting? An infrastructure simulator is read by people scanning dense state and relationships. That argues for clarity, stable layout, and restrained color that leaves room for status signals. Let the subject matter, not a trend, drive the choice.
2. **Pick one bold axis.** Typography, color, or layout. Decide which one carries the identity. The other two stay conventional and quiet.
3. **Write the tokens.** Typefaces with roles, 4 to 6 named colors, a spacing scale, a radius value, a shadow policy (often none). Put them in the Project direction section of SKILL.md.
4. **State the defining constraint.** One sentence a teammate could use to reject an off-brand choice, for example "no color except for state" or "everything aligns to a 12-column grid, no floating cards".
5. **Review against the defaults below.** If the plan matches one of these clusters, it is not a direction yet. Change the bold axis or the palette until it reads as chosen for this product.

## Generic defaults to avoid

These are the recognizable outputs of untargeted generation. Avoid landing in any of them by accident.

- **Warm editorial**: cream background, terracotta or rust accent, serif display font, generous whitespace.
- **Dark neon**: near-black background, one acid-bright accent (lime, cyan, magenta), glow effects, monospace everywhere.
- **Broadsheet**: hairline rules, tiny tracked all-caps labels, numbered sections (01, 02, 03) on content that is not sequential.
- **SaaS dashboard**: white cards with soft shadows on a light gray background, rounded corners, blue primary button, icon-in-a-tinted-circle stat tiles.
- **Template chrome**: gradient hero, three-column feature grid with icons, testimonial carousel, purple-to-blue gradient text.

## Signs of an intentional direction

- The palette could not be swapped for another product's without the design getting worse.
- The type choice was made for the reading task (dense tables, long prose, quick scanning), not for mood alone.
- Layout structure follows the information's actual shape: hierarchy, sequence, relationships.
- Removing any single decorative element would not be noticed, because there are none.
